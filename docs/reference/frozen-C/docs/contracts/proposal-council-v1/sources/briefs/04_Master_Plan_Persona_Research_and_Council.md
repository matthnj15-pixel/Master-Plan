# Master Plan · Persona research and council behavior

## Assignment

Develop fictional people and institutions for Millbrooke whose decisions reflect real planning concerns. Begin in parallel with the read-only audit. Research and draft profiles before game rules or prompt content change. Coordinate the stance and persuasion inputs with the engine contract owner.

## Research method

1. Start from the existing scenario, its landowners, businesses, institutions, and council. Inventory who owns a site, who presents an item, who is affected, and who can vote. Read scenario facts before adding biography or authority.
2. Collect primary sources from comparable Pennsylvania communities. Use adopted planning documents, university and borough material, agricultural extension, and transportation guidance. Capture a concern, what creates it, who bears it, what a credible concession looks like, and how confident we are. Build fictional characters from those incentives. Keep real institutions and individuals out of the game cast.
3. Deliver a source-to-behavior matrix with concrete examples and counterexamples. Mark each item **simulated fact**, **scenario-authored rule**, **character belief**, or **flavor**. If a concern needs a new mechanic, identify the gap and proposed requirement for the engine team. Keep current forecasts grounded in available game data.
4. Start with a compact vertical slice. Cover one farmer or landowner, a college representative, the proposing business or developer, and two contrasting council members. Expand to renters, nearby residents, merchants, and the planning or finance staff when the first slice proves the structure.

## Seed cases to develop

### A farmer protecting productive land

- **Situation.** A redevelopment concept consumes working fields or cuts the farm’s equipment access. This particular owner values the working farm and wants a viable next generation. A tenant farmer, a retiring owner, and a successor may respond differently.
- **Behavior.** Ask which acres are affected, whether fields stay connected, and how equipment reaches them. Consider a smaller footprint on already disturbed land, preserved access, or a different site. Refuse a proposal when it breaks the operation or when an applicable, engine-modeled restriction makes it infeasible. A farmer who supports a farm stand could still oppose a subdivision.
- **Authority.** Tie consent or formal control to recorded ownership and applicable fictional game rules. A concern can influence a negotiation or vote even when the speaker lacks approval authority.
- **Research starting points.** Pennsylvania’s farmland preservation program and Penn State Extension’s farm succession material supply distinct land and household-business concerns. [Pennsylvania Department of Agriculture](https://www.pa.gov/agencies/pda/plants-land-water/farmland-preservation/easement-purchase) · [Penn State Extension](https://extension.psu.edu/business-and-operations/business-management/transition-and-succession-planning)

### A college next to a beer distributor

- **Situation.** A distributor is proposed on a site near a campus pedestrian route or residence hall. Establish what the business actually does. A freight-only warehouse, a public-facing sales counter, and on-site consumption present different concerns.
- **Behavior.** For freight, ask about truck routes and crossing conflicts, loading hours, setbacks, noise, and screening. For a public-facing alcohol use, assess student-facing exposure and campus policies only where relevant. The college might accept daytime deliveries from an industrial road and a screened loading area, or oppose a plan that keeps trucks crossing the busiest campus route. The operator can counter with operational needs and costs.
- **Authority.** The college can raise an objection, request a condition, or decline an agreement within its own authority. Apply a veto only where a scenario rule and facts establish one. Keep a facility's use and operations separate from how a character feels about it.
- **Research starting points.** The Centre Region plan gives town-and-gown land-use context, FHWA provides freight siting considerations, and CDC guidance distinguishes storefront alcohol outlets from wholesale operations without storefronts. Use these as research inputs for fictional conditions. [Centre Region Comprehensive Plan](https://www.crcog.net/vertical/sites/%7B6AD7E2DC-ECE4-41CD-B8E1-BAC6A6336348%7D/uploads/2010_Comprehensive_Plan_%28Complete_Report_with_Resolutions%29_Small_File.pdf) · [FHWA Truck Parking Development Handbook](https://ops.fhwa.dot.gov/freight/infrastructure/truck_parking/docs/Truck_Parking_Development_Handbook.pdf) · [CDC alcohol outlet measurement toolkit](https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/150909/cdc_150909_DS1.pdf)

## Profile to hand to the engine and AI teams

Each profile should define a stable fictional actor ID, role and authority, interests in priority order, affected places or groups, evidence they would ask for, hard constraints and negotiable preferences, acceptable concessions, sensitivity to relationship and relevant recorded history, and concise voice guidance. Define when stance can change and what evidence would justify that change. Keep a separate label for things the character suspects.

A character may agree with the proposal’s goal and still refuse its current design. Give every counteroffer an actionable condition, an owner, and an engine-supported way to test it. A promise requires a recognized game action before any character treats it as a binding commitment. Different actors in the same occupation can have different priorities.

## Acceptance cases

- A farmer supports a stand that preserves field access, counters a layout that cuts access, and declines a version that consumes the working site. The reason references the actual site facts.
- A college reacts differently when loading moves to a non-campus route or when the business adds public sales. A freight-only fixture does not inherit retail alcohol concerns automatically.
- Two council members hear the same true pitch about the same plan and give different grounded reactions. A false claim about an absent feature earns no credit for that claim. The engine applies existing persuasion limits, and the final vote uses the game’s decision rule.
- The same approved candidate and game state produce consistent material outcomes across repeated runs. Voice can vary within the supplied facts. The player can tell what concrete change would improve support.

Return the source matrix, starter profiles, scenario data changes proposed for review, and the cases needed to verify the first roster. Distinguish facts already represented in the engine from new mechanics that need product approval.
